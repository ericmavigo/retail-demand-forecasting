# Analysis and Dashboard Roadmap

This roadmap orders the project by business value. Each stage should be complete and validated before the next one begins.

## Priority 0 — Trusted analytical table

**Goal:** create one reliable table at daily item-store level.

- Reshape daily sales from wide to long format.
- Join calendar, event, SNAP and weekly price data.
- Parse dates and enforce compact data types.
- Separate pre-launch days from genuine zero-demand days.
- Flag missing weekly price coverage instead of inventing prices.
- Validate primary keys, nonnegative units, positive prices and date continuity.
- Create `estimated_revenue = units_sold × sell_price`.
- Save a compact Parquet analytical table and a validation report.

Current evidence:

- 30,490 item-store series and 1,941 sales days.
- 66,927,173 total units sold.
- 67.998% of daily observations are zero; most are valid intermittent demand or pre-launch periods, not automatically dirty data.
- Median first sale occurs on day 160, so product availability must be modeled explicitly.
- No duplicate series IDs, negative unit sales, nonpositive prices or duplicate calendar/price keys were found.

## Priority 1 — Executive performance overview

**Goal:** let a recruiter understand the business in under one minute.

KPI cards:

- Total units sold
- Estimated revenue
- Active products
- Active stores
- Average selling price per unit
- Best-performing store

Charts:

1. Units and estimated revenue by store.
2. Category and department contribution.
3. Year-over-year units and revenue growth.
4. Top 10 products and stores.
5. Pareto curve showing the share generated by the top 20% of products.

## Priority 2 — Five-year seasonality story

**Goal:** explain when demand moves and why.

Charts:

1. Five-year weekly time series with a 28-day moving average.
2. Yearly lines overlaid by week of year.
3. Monthly seasonality by category.
4. Weekday sales pattern.
5. Calendar heatmap by year and week.
6. Best and worst sales weeks.
7. Event and holiday lift versus comparable normal days.
8. SNAP-day lift by eligible state.

## Priority 3 — Store and product diagnostics

**Goal:** show where growth, volatility and operational risk come from.

- Store ranking and trend.
- Store-category matrix.
- Product ABC segmentation by estimated revenue.
- Fast, medium, slow and intermittent demand classification.
- New-product launch cohorts.
- Price-change and demand relationship.
- Volatility and stockout-risk proxy.

## Priority 4 — Forecasting model

**Goal:** beat the 73.86% WAPE baseline on the unchanged 28-day holdout.

- Lag and rolling-demand features.
- Price, event, SNAP and calendar features.
- Global LightGBM model.
- Rolling-origin validation.
- Error by store, category and demand segment.
- Feature importance and SHAP explanations.

## Priority 5 — Inventory decision simulator

**Goal:** turn forecasts into an operational recommendation.

- Reorder point.
- Safety stock.
- Configurable service level.
- Holding-cost and stockout-cost assumptions.
- Comparison of baseline versus machine-learning decisions.
- Estimated reduction in excess inventory and missed sales.

## Metric limitation

M5 contains aggregated daily unit sales, weekly item prices and calendar information. It does not contain transaction or basket identifiers. Therefore, a true **average ticket** cannot be calculated. The project will use correctly labeled alternatives:

- Estimated daily revenue
- Average selling price per unit
- Units sold per store-day
- Revenue per active item-store-day
